## Homepage hero: "Dawn to current"

The first screen is a full-height film behind an editorial introduction: the kicker "PCI Cables · Dankuni, India", the headline (editable through the CMS `home` entry), a supporting line, *Explore our products* and *Request a quote*, a facts strip and a pause/play button.

The film is an original 24-second loop rendered offline in three.js; nothing in it is footage or taken from other videos. `lib/hero-film-scene.ts` assembles six shots. The landscape (`lib/hero-film/countryside.ts`) and the close-up sets (`lib/hero-film/*-shot.ts`) share one finishing pipeline (`lib/hero-film/look.ts`). The models are PCI's own: the ACSR conductor builder, the product-page XLPE cable and the cable-drawn PCI mark (`lib/pci-mark.ts`, shared with the About page). It is illustrative, not footage of a specific installation.

1. **Dawn (0–4 s).** A drone rises over mist lying in the fields as the sun clears the horizon behind a double-circuit line; towers and trees in silhouette.
2. **The crossarm (4–8 s).** A slow crane down a string of toughened-glass cap-and-pin insulators with the dawn sun glowing through the glass, ending on the yoke plate and suspension clamps holding a twin bundle of ACSR.
3. **Inside the conductor (8–12 s).** In a dark studio, the camera tracks beside an ACSR conductor while warm pulses of current race along the aluminium strands, then swings round as the layers telescope apart to the galvanised steel core.
4. **The cable (12–16 s).** PCI's four-core armoured LT XLPE cable turns and opens layer by layer: sheath, steel wire armour, inner sheath, coloured cores, sector-shaped aluminium conductors.
5. **Blue hour (16–20 s).** The same line under a twilight sky, aviation lights on, current flowing down the conductors toward a town on the horizon.
6. **The mark (20–24 s).** A cable draws the PCI mark (PCI and CABLES, without the "Industries Pvt. Ltd." line) on the right of the frame, a glint runs across it, and the film dips back to dawn.

Every shot keeps the left of the frame calm and darker, where the headline sits. The current is shown as a subtle warm glow (`lib/hero-film/pulse.ts`, with selective bloom), not as lightning. The finish: a physical sky (a twilight dome for blue hour), soft shadows, haze and mist (`lib/hero-film/mist.ts`), ambient occlusion, depth of field, drone-like drift, an anamorphic streak from the sun, and a filmic grade with grain, vignette, slight lens distortion and colour fringing. Close-ups use neutral tone mapping so PCI's colours stay true. The web captures retain their native resolution; delivery files use scene-aware intermediate frame blending at 60 fps to soften motion without blending across detected cuts. Full master rendering can additionally average three moments across a 180° shutter. Short dips to navy hide each cut and the loop point.

Homepage delivery: `pci-hero-film-landscape-smooth.mp4/.webm` (1920 × 1080) and `pci-hero-film-portrait-smooth.mp4/.webm` (1080 × 1350), both 24 seconds at 60 fps. The un-suffixed MP4/WebM files retain the 30 fps captures. Matching `pci-hero-film-landscape-smooth.webp` and `pci-hero-film-portrait-smooth.webp` posters are taken from the first captured frame. The new delivery filenames ensure browser and Next image caches cannot reuse the superseded film. The homepage references completed web exports only; unfinished 4K masters are not requested. The poster image is the LCP element; the video uses `preload="none"` and only downloads when it is allowed to play. It pauses off screen and in hidden tabs. Reduced motion and the saved Quick info pause show the poster without downloading the film; the play button still lets a visitor start it. The text stays readable without JavaScript.

To re-render, run the site (`pnpm dev`), then in your own terminal:
- `node scripts/render-hero-film.mjs --web` · native 1080p landscape and 1080 × 1350 portrait captures, one pass per frame; add `--layout=portrait` or `--layout=landscape` to render one format. `--preview` remains landscape only;
- `node scripts/smooth-hero-film.mjs` · finish both captures as 60 fps MP4/WebM delivery files; add `landscape` or `portrait` to finish one format. Intermediate frames are blended with scene-cut detection. This is frame interpolation, not a native 60 fps scene capture;
- `node scripts/render-hero-film.mjs` · the full set: 4K master plus 1080p from the same frames, and the phone film (several hours; add `--layout=landscape` or `--layout=portrait` to split it).

`node scripts/hero-film-stills.mjs [seconds…]` saves review stills and a contact sheet to `test-results/`. Preview the film live, or freeze a frame, at `/hero-film/` (`?t=4.5`); the page is not indexed. Run `node scripts/test-hero.mjs` to check playback, the pause button, off-screen pausing, motion preferences, the phone film, the no-JavaScript poster and accessibility.

## About PCI: inside every connection

The homepage About section tells one story in three beats on a single pinned stage. The shared timeline lives in `lib/about-timeline.ts` and drives both the CSS variables and the Three.js scene.

1. **Anatomy (progress 0–0.38).** A real-time four-core armoured cable lies across the centre of the stage and separates along its length: outer sheath, galvanised steel armour (fanning outwards), inner bedding, colour-coded XLPE cores and stranded aluminium. Five labels sit in fixed slots either side of the artwork, and live leader lines connect each one to its layer as it moves. Outlined background type ("CABLES · CONDUCTORS · EARTH WIRE") drifts with scroll.
2. **Range (0.34–0.70).** The layers close, the artwork settles into the left column and the companion models (single core, aerial bunched cable, bare conductor, earth wire) fly in. A soft orange energy pulse runs along the conductors. The headline fills word by word from outline to solid ink.
3. **Reach (0.68–1).** The story copy appears and the figures count up: 14 products, 17 Indian states, 7 export countries, making cable since 2013. Chapter ticks and a progress bar track the beats along the baseline.

These models and the captured fallback frames are illustrative and are not technical specifications for a PCI product. The original company catalogue documents remain the source for product details. See [ARTWORK.md](ARTWORK.md) for the rendering approach and how to regenerate the fallbacks.

On viewports wider than 820px and taller than 700px, the stage stays beneath the measured header through 240 viewport heights of natural scrolling. Every stage follows scroll position and reverses when scrolling upward. The energy pulse animates only while it is on screen, at about 30 frames per second, and rests after six seconds without scrolling. On smaller screens the finished composition sits in normal flow: the cable, a numbered layer legend, the copy, and the figures. Reduced motion, the saved Quick info motion preference, Skip animation and keyboard focus on any link in the section show the finished composition without the extended scroll area. The full story, including the layer list and figures, is server rendered and remains readable without JavaScript; decorative layers are hidden from assistive technology.

Every stroke is a round cable tube built from the shared centrelines in `lib/pci-logo-geometry.ts`, so face-on it matches the flat logo. ## Turnover: the annual turnover panel

`/turnover/` shows the published financial-year turnover (2015–16 to 2025–26, in ₹ lakh) as a navy panel (`components/turnover.tsx`). A readout gives the selected year in ₹ crore, its value in lakh, and its change on the year before (green for a rise, red for a fall). It starts on the latest year; pointing at, focusing or tapping a bar selects that year, and moving the pointer away returns to the latest. The plot has a ₹ crore scale and a dotted trend line through the bar tops. Three figures sit below it, all computed from the data (35× growth since 2015–16, 43% compound annual growth, and the highest year, FY 2024–25 at ₹277.1 crore), and the full table stays under "View turnover data".

When the bars come into view, they rise year by year, the readout counts up, and the trend line draws across with a spark on its leading edge (about 2.4 s). The entrance plays again after the chart has been scrolled back below the screen. Reduced motion, the Quick info pause and a page without JavaScript show the finished chart. Run `node scripts/test-turnover.mjs` to check the entrance, readout, table, accessibility and the phone layout.

## Featured products: the conductor span

Right after the About story, "Featured products." shows PCI's six overhead conductors (AAC, AAAC, ACSR, AACSR, ACAR, ECO Panther) and a copper control cable hanging from a drawn transmission span between two lattice towers (`components/conductor-line.tsx`). Each product clips onto the wire where it sags and links to its product page. Seven smaller illustrations fit the span, with a copper-coloured connector identifying control cable. The note distinguishes overhead networks from control-cable applications. The section replaces the earlier three photo cards, whose diagrams did not match the site's look.

The towers are proportioned lattice drawings (tapered, braced legs, two crossarms with disc insulator strings, an earth-wire peak and a small warning light) in the section's muted greys; they keep their shape at every width while the wires stretch between them. While the reader scrolls toward the section only the towers show. The span plays once each time it comes to rest fully in view: the line draws from the left tower to the right (1.5 s), each conductor drops onto the wire as the drawing reaches it, the left tower's light glows, a soft orange spark crosses the span lighting each clamp as it passes (1.75–4.35 s), and the right tower's light answers. It settles in under six seconds; scrolling the span fully out of view re-arms it, so it plays again on the next visit. Keyboard focus reveals it immediately. Reduced motion, the saved pause setting and a page without JavaScript show the finished span with no animation. On phones the conductors become a swipeable row of cards. Run `node scripts/test-featured.mjs` to check the links, the entrance, the sag layout, the phone row, motion preferences, no-JS and accessibility.
